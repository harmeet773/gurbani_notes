Rehras Sahib

Rehras Sahib is the evening bani of Sikh daily practice, offering spiritual steadiness, gratitude, and renewed courage at the close of the day.

This page follows the same nested accordion structure as the Japji Sahib page so readers can move from overview to composition, themes, and study notes.

Rehras Sahib gathers shabads that turn the mind toward remembrance, reliance on Divine support, and resilience after the strain of daily life.

  • Tradition: Recited in the evening as part of Nitnem.
  • Sources: Drawn from Guru Granth Sahib and completed in common practice with the concluding portion attributed to Guru Gobind Singh Ji.
  • Character: A bani of reflection, strength, and praise.
  • Role: Helps transition from worldly activity into remembrance and gratitude.

  • Renewal: Recenters the mind after the demands of the day.
  • Strength: Encourages courage, trust, and moral endurance.
  • Devotion: Rekindles praise and remembrance through sung wisdom.
  • Balance: Links work in the world with spiritual grounding.

  • Commonly recited at sunset or in the evening.
  • Used to cultivate gratitude and spiritual composure after daily labor.
  • Often approached as a bani of support during fatigue, pressure, or uncertainty.

  • Begins with praise and dependence on the Divine rather than the self.
  • Moves through shabads that emphasize support, refuge, and remembrance.
  • Builds an emotional arc from weariness toward confidence and devotion.

  1. The bani brings together passages associated with different Gurus within a single devotional movement.
  2. This combination gives Rehras Sahib a layered tone of praise, surrender, and disciplined courage.
  3. Its compiled form makes it especially suitable for collective daily recitation.

  • The ending intensifies the note of confidence in Divine protection and power.
  • It leaves the reciter with a sense of steadiness rather than exhaustion.
  • The overall flow turns the close of day into an act of conscious remembrance.

Rehras Sahib repeatedly reminds the reciter that real support does not come from status, possessions, or personal control, but from Divine care and guidance.

The bani frames the evening as a time to acknowledge blessings, remember the Giver, and redirect attention away from ego and anxiety.

Its verses strengthen the inner posture needed to face difficulty without panic, helping devotion function as a source of resolve.

Rehras Sahib does not reject worldly responsibility. It teaches how remembrance can remain active even after a full day of effort, fatigue, and distraction.

  • Read it as an evening transition rather than only a fixed obligation.
  • Notice how the bani answers fatigue with praise, trust, and steadiness.
  • Track repeated language of refuge, support, and Divine greatness.
